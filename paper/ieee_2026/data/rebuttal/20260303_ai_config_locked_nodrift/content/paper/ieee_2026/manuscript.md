# MIC Self-Learning Control for Induction Motors: Reproducible Benchmark on Three Motors

## Abstract
This paper presents a reproducible benchmark of `PI`, `FOC`, and `MIC` controllers for three induction motors (`AIR56`, `AL31`, `AO2`) in a physical digital twin. Evaluation uses fixed scenarios (`speed_step`, `ramp`, `load_step`, `start_stop`) and fixed seeds (`101,202,303,404,505`). The frozen package `20260303_ai_config_locked_nodrift` shows stable MIC energy benefit over FOC/PI in both IEEE modes: mean input-power saving `0.9176%` with non-negative efficiency gain (`+0.0715%`) while preserving speed-control constraints.

## I. Introduction
Industrial deployment requires two properties simultaneously: energy efficiency and reproducibility. We therefore evaluate MIC not by one-off plots, but by a fixed protocol with frozen seeds, scenario set, and acceptance gates on three motors with different ratings.

## II. Method
1. Plant: asynchronous motor digital twin with physical losses.
2. Controllers: PI baseline, FOC baseline, MIC (improved regulator).
3. Protocol:
   - motors: `AIR56`, `AL31`, `AO2`;
   - scenarios: `speed_step,ramp,load_step,start_stop`;
   - seeds: `101,202,303,404,505`.
4. Metrics:
   - `avg_power_saving_pct`,
   - `avg_eta_gain_pct`,
   - `err_failures`,
   - `start_stop_power_saving_pct`.
5. Acceptance gates are generated automatically by `tools/reproduce_ieee_step28.py`.

## IV. Results
For both mode1 (`FOC encoder` vs `MIC sensorless`) and mode2 (`FOC sensorless` vs `MIC sensorless`), frozen table values are identical:
1. MIC mean power saving: `0.9176%` (std `0.8486%`, minimum across motors `0.0918%`).
2. MIC mean eta gain: `+0.0715%` (minimum `+0.0067%`).
3. Error failures: mean `0.6667`, worst-case `2`.
4. Mean `start_stop` saving: `2.6697%`.

Per-motor acceptance summary:
1. `AIR56`: saving `0.5763%`, eta gain `+0.1887%`, start_stop `-0.2117%`, acceptance `PASS`.
2. `AL31`: saving `2.0846%`, eta gain `+0.0067%`, start_stop `8.2348%`, acceptance `PASS`.
3. `AO2`: saving `0.0918%`, eta gain `+0.0191%`, start_stop `-0.0139%`, acceptance `PASS`.

## V. Theory Validation
The package includes automatic theory checks (`theory_validation_report.json`):
1. `0 <= cosphi <= 1`
2. `0 <= eta <= 1.02`
3. `P2 <= P1_total + eps`
4. shape checks for `M2`, `I1`, `n2`, `eta`, `cosphi`.

## VI. Discussion
MIC provides consistent positive energy effect across motors with different nominal power. The largest gain is observed for AL31, while AO2 remains positive but with low margin and should be monitored in future tuning cycles. This behavior supports a robust-but-not-uniform transfer across motor classes.

## VII. Conclusion
The project now has an IEEE-ready reproducible pipeline with frozen metrics, automatic validation, and publication artifacts. MIC demonstrates stable efficiency advantage over PI/FOC under fixed seeds and scenarios on three motors.
